### Cause and evidence

The incident meets the **faulty source step with the values that reached it** standard. The saved log gives a current time of `0x1011593fc4e3c0` and a deadline of `0x1011588a08d780`. The deadline was already **3.049 seconds overdue**. The GH100 teardown HAL used by this Blackwell GPU supplied flags zero to the GA102 RISC-V halt poll. The first unsuccessful HALTED read could therefore time out immediately. The log does not establish how long the firmware took to halt, whether it eventually halted, or which allocation consumed the time. [Incident evidence](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/nvidia-report.md) · [GH100 caller](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/baseline/src/nvidia/src/kernel/gpu/gsp/arch/hopper/kernel_gsp_gh100.c:1111) · [GA102 poll](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/baseline/src/nvidia/src/kernel/gpu/falcon/arch/ampere/kernel_falcon_ga102.c:277) · [RM timeout selection](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/baseline/src/nvidia/src/kernel/gpu/gpu_timeout.c:492)

I ran `/usr/bin/python3 tests/run-halt-test.py baseline verified`. It compiled NVIDIA’s actual timeout setup, local timeout check, timeout dispatch, GA102 poll and GH100 caller. The register, clock and parent deadline were test inputs.

A normal resume lacked a trustworthy saved state. I also found that the mailbox error could be overwritten by final teardown and that the Unix wrapper discarded the return from `gpuResumeFromStandby`. ### Recovery and the remaining release blocker

**Before engine unload**, rollback is realistic. The stage tests covered failed framebuffer reservation, failed SR metadata, an expired budget and the one-shot test parameter. All four ended at depth zero with interrupts restored, no engine unload and no GSP suspend command. **From engine pre-unload onward**, the conservative outcome is restart-required. The depth enum cannot identify every engine already changed. `gpuStateUnload` also records and tolerates some engine errors. Clearing standby flags and calling resume would risk admitting work to a partially restored GPU. [Stage results](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/tests/stage-results.txt) · [Engine unload implementation](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/baseline/src/nvidia/src/kernel/gpu/gpu.c:3818)

**Linux should receive `-EAGAIN` or `-EBUSY` only when the rejected suspend leaves the device operational.** On a synthetic device under the exact guest kernel, both left `runtime_error=0`. `-EIO` left `runtime_error=-5` with bookkeeping status active, and ordinary resume returned `-22`. Calling `pm_runtime_set_active()` on the independently known-active fixture cleared the error, after which a complete suspend/resume passed. For real recovery, serialize PM, restore and verify hardware first, then reconcile PM status and check the setter’s return. Calling it inside the failing callback precedes the core’s error recording. [Guest output](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/tests/guest-final.txt) · [Linux error recording](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/linux-source/linux-7.2.15/drivers/base/power/runtime.c:472) · [Status setter](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/linux-source/linux-7.2.15/drivers/base/power/runtime.c:1313)

**The missing guarantee is DMA quiescence.** After an ambiguous GSP command, firmware may still own a save target. The stock error path can free FBSR backing. Later, `nv_stop_device` takes a power reference, `rm_cleanup_dynamic_power_management` can attempt a wake, `RmDisableAdapter` can unload engines, and `RmShutdownAdapter` destroys clients, rings and mappings. `gpuStateDestroy` can request GSP unload again. A rejected RPC or expired display timer establishes none of the conditions needed to reuse DMA memory. Existing BAR mappings, IRQ work, fences and importers also outlive a new-open gate. [Removal and destructor analysis](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/recovery-design.md) · [RM shutdown](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/baseline/src/nvidia/arch/nvalloc/unix/src/osinit.c:2392) · [Second GSP unload](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/baseline/src/nvidia/src/kernel/gpu/gpu.c:4170)

The required implementation is a per-binding lifetime that can survive unplug, with admission closed, mappings revoked, waiters completed with errors, workers synchronized and backing retained until a vendor-qualified fence or reset succeeds. Only then may host-only destructors free the old generation and a fresh probe admit clients. Linux’s PCI remove callback cannot return “busy.” An administrative maintenance request **can** return a bounded failure before starting an unbind it cannot finish. The saved `reset_method` advertised `flr bus`. Whether FLR restores this board and its GSP remains a hardware question. A bus reset also affects the HDA sibling. [Recovery contract](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/qualification.md) · [Linux PCI remove](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/linux-source/linux-7.2.15/drivers/pci/pci-driver.c:523)

The wait audit covers the EVO ring and notifiers, channel idle, cursor PIO, GPFIFO, connector retry, DisplayPort, DRM flips and fences, and worker joins. Two important residual waits are DRM semaphore callback re-registration and the worker completion behind `cancel_delayed_work_sync` or queue shutdown. There is **no verified whole-teardown deadline** in this series. [Wait-by-wait disposition](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/wait-audit.md) · [1,313 indexed loop and wait sites](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/tests/loop-index.tsv)

### Save-buffer allocation

The GSP save buffer **could be reserved after firmware layout discovery**. Its size depends on `srRegionsInfo`, populated after GSP initialization. The radix allocation includes page tables and payload, tries 2 MiB pages and falls back to 4 KiB. Metadata is allocated and mapped separately. Reuse needs a firmware/layout/DMA-generation check and fresh metadata on every cycle. A failed restore must retain anything firmware might still access. FBSR separately reserves the currently used framebuffer size and can allocate bookkeeping nodes. Caching only the GSP buffer therefore cannot make suspend allocation-free. I left permanent caching out of the series pending measurements of retained memory on this GPU. [Layout discovery](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/baseline/src/nvidia/src/kernel/gpu/gsp/kernel_gsp.c:5794) · [SR preparation](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/baseline/src/nvidia/src/kernel/gpu/gsp/arch/turing/kernel_gsp_tu102.c:1127) · [Cache design](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/recovery-design.md)

### Hardware qualification

1. Start on a healthy spare board with Intel driving the console and a complete stock rollback boot. Verify the loaded build IDs, expected compute checksum, connector roles and a normal GCOFF suspend/resume cycle.
2. Trial **0001–0003** first. Capture send, mailbox, HALTED timing and resume result. A late HALTED followed by correct computation would support deadline exhaustion as sufficient to explain the incident. A busy register throughout the fresh interval would point to firmware or board behavior.
3. On the qualification build, arm `test_defer_gcoff_minor` once for the selected minor. Verify the preflight message, automatic disarm to `-1`, `-EAGAIN`, clear `runtime_error`, immediate correct computation and a later successful ordinary cycle. GC6 does not exercise that hook.
4. Test healthy DRM close and reopen, UVM, dma-buf export/import, framebuffer ownership, hotplug and an actual NVIDIA-driven output. Record final references, worker exits and error-fence delivery.
5. **Late halt, frozen GET and post-engine failures were injected only into hardware-free fixtures.** Physical late-fault injection is outstanding because the current destructors can free memory before DMA quiescence is established. Acceptance on a spare card requires verified isolation, a bounded maintenance outcome, a fresh probe and a correct new workload. If a kernel operation remains pending, preserve that transaction and recover through the known-good boot, with a full power cycle if necessary. [Step-by-step test and recovery plan](/var/tmp/claude-blueferry/nvfork/qualification.md)

**My release order is the no-wake fixes and displayless `fbdev=0` qualification first, then the narrow prevention fork on spare hardware, then the reference-lifetime patches.** The full recovery and display-timeout series belongs with NVIDIA for review before deployment on the only card. The hard part that remains is proving when the old GPU generation has lost the ability to write memory.
