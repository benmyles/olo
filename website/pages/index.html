Free A desktop for Linux, in the spirit of BeOS

The desktop that never makes you wait.

Olo is a whole desktop, written in Rust for Wayland. BeOS's yellow window tabs, Deskbar and Tracker's spatial folders, brought to 2026: crisp at every scale, light, dark and night, and made for you and the AI agents working beside you.

Why Olo

Everything BeOS got right, and what 2026 does better.

Olo keeps what made BeOS a joy, a desktop you understand and that answers at once, and takes the better way wherever there is one.

{{icon performance 64}}

Fast and light, measured

Idle costs nothing: no polling, no frames when nothing changed. Anything that can take more than a frame runs on a worker thread with live progress. Every change to what runs per frame is measured before and after.

{{icon application 64}}

Native, all the way down

The window server, the kit and every app are Olo's own, in Rust, sharing one kit. Formats and protocols are read and spoken in process, so the whole desktop looks and behaves as one.

{{icon agent-screens 64}}

Made for agents too

What you can see, an agent can read as structure and drive through a documented API, on screens of its own that you can watch live and take over, never touching your pointer.

The desktop

Familiar the moment you see it. Quick the moment you touch it.

Deskbar and Tracker, as they should be

Deskbar sits in the top-right corner, as on BeOS: the Olo menu, the clock, a tray, and each workspace's running apps. Tracker is the desktop and its folders, spatial, with Quick Look for almost any file.

  • Live widgets in Deskbar: processor, memory, GPUs, disks, network, temperatures and power, as graphs, bars, meters or numbers.
  • Icon, list and column views, each folder opening where you left it.
  • Quick Look previews of pictures, PDFs, video, audio, documents and archives.
{{shot olo-tracker | Tracker: a folder of pictures as icons, and Quick Look on one of them}}

A command palette for everything

Press ⌘ Space and type: apps, the front app's menu commands, open windows, files, settings and the web, all in one place.

Keyboard hints put a letter on every control in every window, so anything you can click, you can reach from the keyboard.

{{shot olo-palette | The command palette, finding Terminal}}

Workspaces, the overview and tiling

A set of workspaces for each display, and an overview of them all. Arrange windows from the keyboard, turn on auto-tiling, keep a window group in tabs, or let Smart Tiling place things for you.

Two themes, light, dark and night

Classic, after BeOS, and Platinum, after Mac OS 9: each draws every icon, window and control in its own style, with a wallpaper to match. Night mode warms the screen on a schedule, gradually if you like.

Pixel-exact at 1×, 1.5×, 2× and every scale between, with HDR and wide-gamut colour, variable refresh rate, and each display on its own GPU.

{{shot olo-themes | The same desktop in Classic and Platinum, light and dark}}

Your phone, natively

Pair an iPhone or an Android phone over Bluetooth and its messages and contacts are on your desktop. Olo speaks to the phone itself: nothing to install on the phone, no account, no cloud between them.

  • Pairing in three steps, each seen to happen: find the phone, confirm the code, allow it on the phone.
  • Phone, an app for your conversations and contacts: read, answer, start a new one.
  • One-time codes texted to you are offered in the browser tab that's waiting for one, or in an agent's tab, with your say.
  • Watched: it reconnects by itself, and Olo Doctor says so if it can't.

Made for agents too

Your agents get a desktop of their own.

Agent-first, as much as people-first: everything you can see and do, an agent can read and drive, without getting in your way.

  • Agent Screens: whole headless Olo desktops for agents, which you can watch live, scrub back through, and take over at any moment.
  • Structured, not pixels: every window's widgets as a tree, every menu command by name, and an API to click, type and choose.
  • Coding agents in Terminal title their own tabs, are kept going while you sleep and get worktrees made for them: more below.
  • Token Pool shares several Claude and ChatGPT subscriptions among them, with no gap across upgrades.
$ olo-screen start
$ olo-screen run agent olo-styled-edit notes.md
$ olo-screen tree agent --role Button
$ olo-screen click agent --role Button --name Save

Terminal knows its coding agents

Run Claude Code or Codex in a tab and Terminal sees it there: the tab says whether the agent is working, waiting for you or needs an answer, and a notification tells you when it does.

  • Titles kept by the agent: each agent gets a tool to title its tab with what it's working on, and keeps it current, so a row of tabs reads as a list of what's under way.
  • Tab groups, named and coloured, one per feature or per repository; a window whose tabs are all in one repository becomes its group by itself.
  • Told where it is as it starts: the group's name and every repository and worktree its tabs are in, its own marked. The picture shows the very words.
{{shot olo-terminal-agents | Terminal: a tab group named Refunds, three tabs titled by their agents, and what an agent is told as it starts in one}}

Keep It Going

An agent that stops at midnight has stopped until morning. Turn on Keep It Going in its tab's menu and Terminal types “Please continue” for you.

  • Only when it has really stopped: two quiet minutes, its turn over and its own prompt showing; never while it works, never on a question, at most once an hour.
  • Your words: a message of your own for every tab, or for one agent alone.
  • Kept with the tab through upgrades and restarts.
{{shot olo-terminal-agent-menu | A tab's menu in Terminal, with Keep It Going's own menu open: On, the default message, a custom message}}

Auto Approve

“Do you want to proceed?” needn't wait for you either. With Auto Approve on, Terminal answers Yes thirty seconds after an agent asks leave to go on.

  • A countdown you can stop in the tab's corner; typing in the tab makes the question yours.
  • Only what asks leave: not a plan waiting to be approved, not another kind of question.
  • Yours to turn on: an agent that asks for it gets an alert on your screen whose Return is Cancel, so it can't answer its own questions.
{{shot olo-terminal-auto-approve | Terminal: an agent's stand-in asking whether to proceed, and Auto Approve counting down to Yes with a Stop button}}

Worktrees and pull requests, per tab

An agent per worktree is how several work on one repository at once. Terminal makes them for you and shows where each stands.

  • New Worktree…: a name, which is a folder and a branch both, made where you keep them and opened in a new tab of the group. Rename and remove them from the tab's menu too.
  • Git in process: no git command to shell out to, and nothing polled; a tab follows its folder as it changes.
  • Each tab wears its pull request: open, draft, merged or closed, and whether its checks pass, fail or run.
{{shot olo-terminal-worktree | Terminal's New Worktree window: a name typed, and where its folder and branch will be made}}

You say what agents are told

Everything Terminal tells an agent is a setting, off until you turn it on, in Preferences ▸ Terminal.

  • Screens of their own: agents are told to test desktop apps and web pages on an Agent Screen, never on yours. A tab moves to an agent's screen from its menu, its shell still running.
  • Scratch on disk: where /tmp is held in memory, agents are told to keep their builds and downloads out of it.
  • Set up for you: Terminal puts its hook and its tool in Claude Code's and Codex's own settings, every other setting of theirs as it was, and takes them out when you turn it off.

Fast and light

Rules, not hopes.

Speed isn't a feature added at the end. Olo holds every change to a few rules, and measures it against them.

1

Idle costs nothing

No polling, no frames or events when nothing changed. Work follows what changed, not what's on screen, and work done once is kept.

2

Never block the UI

Anything that can take more than a frame runs on a worker thread, shows live progress, streams its results and can be cancelled.

3

Measured, every time

Every change to what runs per frame, per event or at rest is measured on release builds before and after, latency included, and the numbers go in its commit.

4

Pixel-perfect at every scale

Checked in light, dark and night at 1×, 1.5× and 2×. Nothing drawn from a bitmap is resampled: one texture pixel to one device pixel.

Try Olo today.

On Fedora 44 or 45 Workstation, one program installs it beside GNOME. Choose Olo or GNOME each time you log in.

$ curl -LO https://github.com/benmyles/olo/releases/latest/download/olo-installer-x86_64
